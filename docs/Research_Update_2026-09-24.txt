Research Update
24 September 2026


WHAT WE SET OUT TO TEST

The thesis holds that data orchestration rather than model inference is the limiting factor in
distributed agentic pipelines, and that expressing pipeline stages as collective operations over a
shared columnar format removes that limit. Two formal statements carry it. One bounds the bytes
that must cross operator boundaries. The other predicts that end to end speedup does not decline as
workers are added. This period was spent testing whether our astronomy workload can evaluate
either one.


FIRST, WHAT THE RETRIEVE OPERATOR ACTUALLY DOES

One definition is needed before the result makes sense, because the mechanism is easy to picture
wrongly.

Cosmic AI contains no search component. There is no index and no vector database. What the Retrieve
operator does is decide whether a galaxy's question has already been answered closely enough by a
previous one. It does that by taking the new galaxy's prompt embedding and comparing it against
every embedding already stored, one by one, keeping any whose cosine similarity exceeds a
threshold. This is an exhaustive comparison rather than a lookup. Every stored entry is touched on
every galaxy.

Two quantities follow, and the rest of this section depends on telling them apart. The cost of one
reuse decision is the number of stored embeddings multiplied by the cost of one comparison, which
we measured at 11.1 microseconds. The number of stored embeddings is a separate thing entirely, and
it is not the number of galaxies.


A CLAIM WE HELD DID NOT SURVIVE

We expected stored context to accumulate until one worker could no longer compare against all of it
on every galaxy. The remedy would have been to divide the stored embeddings across workers so each
compares against only its share, with a collective merging the partial results. That expectation is
now withdrawn.

The cost of dividing the work is proportional to how much there is to divide, so everything turns
on how large the stored set becomes. It does not become large. Stored embeddings are not galaxies,
because a galaxy whose prompt finds an acceptable match reuses that answer and stores nothing, on
the reasoning that a duplicate adds no information. Only the ones that find no match are stored.

That makes growth self limiting. A larger stored set satisfies more galaxies, which produces fewer
new entries, which slows growth further. Across the full 1,253 galaxy population the reuse rate
climbs from 58 percent in the first windows to about 93 percent in the last, and the stored set
reaches 146 entries rather than 1,253.

Fitting that trajectory, a square root law describes it better than a linear one. The exponent is
an empirical least squares fit of the logarithm of stored entries against the logarithm of galaxies
seen, which comes out at 0.498, and it explains 97.8 percent of the variance against 92.3 percent
for the linear fit. A sublinear exponent is not surprising for a process of this kind. Discovering
distinct items gets harder as coverage improves, which is the same shape as vocabulary growth in
text, where new words appear at a decreasing rate as more is read.

The two laws agree closely over the range we measured and diverge sharply outside it. Extrapolated
across the entire SDSS survey of roughly 2.6 million galaxies, the linear law predicts about
222,000 stored entries while the square root law predicts about 6,800. At 222,000 entries a reuse
decision costs 2.5 seconds against a model call of 3.3, and dividing the work across workers would
be essential. At 6,800 entries it costs 76 milliseconds, which is about two percent of a model
call, and dividing the work would save nothing worth a collective operation on every galaxy.

We fitted both laws, adopted the one that disfavours our position because it described the data
better, and changed the plan rather than the interpretation.

There is an irony in this worth stating, since it is the most interesting part. Reuse and
partitioned retrieval were both intended contributions, and the first destroys the case for the
second. The better reuse works, the less context accumulates, and the less there is to divide.


THE STRUCTURAL REASON, WHICH GENERALISES

Cosmic AI divides its input, not its state. Each worker takes a contiguous block of galaxies and
carries each one from beginning to end, so almost nothing crosses an operator boundary. The entire
cross worker data plane is one collective carrying about 281 KB per worker per run. A workload of
that shape cannot discriminate between communication designs, because there is no communication to
speak of.

This is the more useful finding, and following it led somewhere better than a workaround.


THE PER OBJECT FRAMING DOES NOT SURVIVE SCALE

Cosmic AI issues one model call per galaxy, to comment on that galaxy's redshift prediction. Two
measurements say this is the wrong shape for analysis at catalogue scale, and neither is a
criticism of the implementation.

The science is a small part of the work. Inference costs 0.134 seconds per galaxy against a model
call of 3.31 seconds, so on a measured run the redshift prediction was under 8 percent of runtime
and commentary about it was 92 percent.

The commentary carries little information about the individual galaxy. That is what the reuse
numbers have been telling us. Without the validity criterion, 99.6 percent of galaxies accept one
of five stored answers. With it, 88 percent accept one of 146. A task in which eight of nine
objects take an interchangeable answer produces an output that is nearly constant across its input.
The reuse rate is measuring redundancy in the task design rather than an optimisation we achieved.

The questions an astronomer asks at survey scale are not per object anyway. How residuals are
distributed, which subpopulations carry systematic bias, which outliers are physically interesting
rather than instrumental. Those are aggregations across the catalogue, and no worker can answer them
from its own block alone.


A WORKLOAD THAT FITS BOTH THE SCIENCE AND THE CLAIM

Population scale anomaly detection over the same data, comparing each galaxy against the
distribution of its own subpopulation rather than analysing it in isolation. This is the analysis
the source project was organised around.

It maps onto the operator abstraction without forcing. Scatter the catalogue, run inference
locally, redistribute by subpopulation and reduce the partial statistics, then characterise the
summaries. The reduce is intrinsic, because a population statistic cannot be computed by one worker
from its own block. Every previous attempt to exercise that mapping felt contrived because it meant
moving state a worker had produced itself and could have used alone. Here nothing is moved that its
owner could have used alone.

One design decision determines whether the experiment has a signal, and it is worth stating
plainly. The redistribution must carry feature vectors rather than summary scalars. Scalars move
about 166 MB across the whole catalogue, which is only nine times what the current workload moves
and would produce a weak result, because aggregation compresses. Comparing a galaxy against its
subpopulation requires the features that define the comparison, which at 1024 dimensions is roughly
10.6 GB across the catalogue, four orders of magnitude above where we are now.

The stateless comparison is the design the serverless literature criticises, where every worker
writes its intermediate features to object storage and reads back the fragments for the
subpopulations it owns. Both arms compute identically and differ only in how the redistribution
happens.

The application gains as well. Across the full survey the population framing needs about 100 model
calls rather than 304,200, which is 5.5 minutes of model time rather than 4.4 hours at 64 workers,
and about two cents rather than fifty eight dollars. Inference then accounts for roughly 94 percent
of runtime, which is the honest shape for an astronomy pipeline.


A DEPENDENCY WE HAD NOT EXAMINED

Every reuse number we have reported rests on a validity criterion whose correctness we have never
tested. Removing the criterion and accepting any sufficiently similar stored context raises reuse
to 99.6 percent and collapses the store to five entries, because the prompts are template generated
and two in five pairs already exceed the similarity threshold. With the criterion, reuse is 88.4
percent and the store reaches 146.

So the criterion, not the embedding, determines the result. Its tolerance is presently the model's
own median prediction error, which our own documentation describes as exploratory rather than a
validated statement of equivalence. We have not compared an accepted reuse against fresh inference
on the same input. Until we do, reuse should be reported as acceptance under a stated policy and
never as demonstrated correctness. This is now the highest value measurement outstanding.


CHOOSING A COMPARATOR THAT ANSWERS THE FEEDBACK

The feedback we received was that end to end speedup had been measured against a weak comparator,
and that the resulting figure therefore proves little. We accept that. Our current headline number
is measured against an HTTP and LangChain arm, which is a reasonable diagnostic for locating where
time is spent but is not a serious opponent for a claim about moving data efficiently.

Identifying a serious opponent required separating two layers that are easily confused, since both
are described in the literature as orchestration.

The first layer is agent orchestration, meaning which agent acts, in what role, and in what order.
LangChain, LangGraph, CrewAI, AutoGen and Haystack occupy it. We surveyed these and concluded that
none of them is a candidate here, because none has a distributed data plane at all. They delegate
execution and pass state through ordinary objects or an external store. Comparing against them
again would answer the same diagnostic question we have already answered.

The second layer is distributed execution and the movement of data between stages. This is the
layer our contribution occupies, and Ray is the state of the art in it. That is the reason we chose
Ray rather than any of the agent frameworks, and it is a direct response to the feedback.

Ray is also the hardest honest opponent available rather than a convenient one, for a reason worth
making explicit. Its shared object store, Plasma, originated inside Apache Arrow and provides zero
copy reads of Arrow formatted data in shared memory. We are therefore not comparing Arrow against
something slower than Arrow. We are comparing two ways of moving Arrow data, against a system that
already possesses the property our thesis claims as its contribution. A favourable result against
that opponent means something. An unfavourable one would be informative rather than merely
disappointing.


A METHODOLOGICAL REVISION, AND WHY CYLONFLOW MATTERS

Adding Ray is necessary but not sufficient. A favourable result against any single system remains
ambiguous between two explanations, a better data plane and a lighter scheduler, and the thesis
claims the former.

We have therefore arranged three configurations as a factorial rather than a list of baselines. The
first is Ray with its own object store. The third is our runtime. The second holds the scheduler
constant, using Ray to place and manage workers while our collectives move the data, so that the
comparison between the second and third isolates the scheduler and the comparison between the first
and second isolates the data plane. The first and third give the headline figure. The second is
what licenses the attribution, and attribution is the actual claim.

The second configuration is possible because the work already exists in part. CylonFlow, developed
within the Cylon project, is an actor framework with executors for Ray, Dask and Parsl, and its Ray
executor already places Cylon workers inside Ray actors. We are adopting its structure rather than
inventing one, which also means the middle arm has lineage within the project rather than being an
artefact we constructed to make a point.

One adaptation is required and is important to state. CylonFlow builds its Cylon environment over
Gloo, while our runtime uses a different transport. Taking it unchanged would vary transport and
scheduler at the same time, which would destroy the isolation that justifies the arm. We are
therefore configuring it with our own transport so that exactly one factor changes between the
second and third configurations. CylonFlow has been dormant since 2022, so this is a port and a
modernisation rather than a dependency.

We expect to report a null result on this workload, since model calls dominate its runtime, and we
would rather predict that in advance than explain it afterwards.


POSITION RELATIVE TO ADJACENT WORK IN THE GROUP

Two recent papers from this group, AAFLOW and its stateful extension, also model agentic workflows
as operators and also build on Arrow and Cylon. The distinction is the object being optimised.
Those systems accelerate inference, the first by removing serialisation between preprocessing,
embedding and retrieval, the second by treating attention state as a distributed object so agents
need not recompute shared context. This work holds inference constant by design and addresses the
communication schedule between stages instead. One of our hypotheses states that generation
throughput is unchanged, and that hypothesis is the boundary marker between the two lines of work.
It should be reported as such rather than left implicit, since the surface similarity is high and a
reader comparing speedup figures across the papers would otherwise be comparing different
quantities.


REVISED POSITION

The bound on data movement is currently unsupported by any experiment on a workload capable of
demonstrating it. That gap is now identified, and a suitable workload is specified.

The scaling prediction remains testable on the present workload.

The reuse results stand as acceptance rates under a policy we have not yet validated.


WHAT I WOULD VALUE FEEDBACK ON

Whether the three way design is the right instrument for separating a data plane effect from a
scheduling effect, and whether the validity criterion should be settled before or alongside the
communication work.

I am also interested in whether the negative result reads as a loss or as a narrowing, since I
consider it the latter.
